## Vision & Mission — read this before anything else

The host doesn't need to memorize the script below word-for-word. He needs to know *why* Pandas LP
exists well enough that every unscripted answer during Q&A still lands on-message.

**Vision:** A Nigeria where being owed money, or being shut out of a fair legal process, isn't
something people just absorb and move on from — because real legal help stopped being a luxury
reserved for those who can afford ₦50,000 and three weeks of waiting.

**Mission:** Pandas LP pairs AI-driven intake with licensed Nigerian lawyers so that starting a real
legal case — a demand letter, a court filing, a judgment enforced — takes minutes and costs from
₦10,000, not weeks and half a million naira. Every document is drafted by AI and reviewed, signed, and
stood behind by a real, licensed lawyer before it ever leaves the building.

**What this means for hosting tonight:**
- Every answer should trace back to one idea: the *wait* is the enemy — not the debtor, not the
  courts, the wait, and everything that grows in the space it leaves.
- When Q&A pushes on "is this really a law firm," the answer is always the mission, not defensiveness:
  we exist specifically so licensed legal help stops being rationed by cost and time.
- Keep the AI in its place in every sentence — it drafts, it doesn't decide, sign, or replace the
  lawyer's review. That's not a legal disclaimer, it's the actual design of the mission — say it like
  you mean it, because it's true.
- The host's job tonight isn't to sell a product. It's to demonstrate the mission working, live, in
  front of witnesses.

### 0:00–0:03 — Cold open

Don't wait for a crowd to build before starting content — the first 20 seconds is what gets clipped
and re-shared, so open with the hook, not housekeeping.

> **Host:** "If someone owes you money right now — and they've stopped answering — this Space is for
> you. I'm [name], founder of Pandas LP. In the next hour my colleague [lawyer's name] and I are going
> to walk you through exactly what happens when you take someone to Small Claims Court in Nigeria — no
> jargon — and then I'm going to show you something we're launching tonight that does the first step
> for you in about ten minutes."

### 0:08–0:18 — The problem, told live

This is the same emotional beat as Scene 1–2 of the brand film — reuse it verbatim, it's already
tested copy, just spoken instead of shot.

> **Host:** "Let me set the scene, and [lawyer], jump in wherever. Say someone — call him Obi, runs a
> small furniture workshop in Lagos — delivers a ₦450,000 dining set. Client says 'I'll pay you next
> week.' That was three months ago. Grey ticks on WhatsApp. Calls ring out. [Lawyer], what actually
> happens if Obi picks up the phone and calls a traditional lawyer today?"

Prompt the lawyer to speak to: typical retainer cost (the film cites ₦50,000+ to start), typical
timeline (2–4 weeks before anything moves), and why most people just... don't, and eat the loss.

> **Host:** "That waiting is exactly what the person who owes the money is counting on. So — what
> actually changes that?"

### 0:18–0:35 — The journey, step by step

Hand this segment to the lawyer. Post the journey graphic in the Space's card / pin it now if you
haven't. Five beats, matching the graphic exactly — keep each to 2–3 minutes, plain English, no statute
citations unless someone asks:

| # | Step | Prompt for the lawyer |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Demand Letter** | "What does a real Letter of Demand actually need to say to hold up? What makes one weak?" |
| 02 | **File & Evidence** | "If the letter's ignored, what evidence actually matters — invoices, WhatsApp messages, what?" (Cue: WhatsApp messages are admissible — same line as the film.) |
| 03 | **Hearing** | "What's a Small Claims hearing actually like? Do you need to hire a barrister, show up in a wig?" (Answer should deflate the intimidation factor — that's the point.) |
| 04 | **Judgment** | "Say you win — then what? Does the money just appear?" |
| 05 | **Enforcement** | "This is the step nobody talks about — what happens when they still don't pay after judgment?" |

> **Host (transition after step 5):** "So that's the real journey — five steps, and most people never
> get past step one because starting it feels like a wall. That's the exact wall we built something to
> remove. Let me show you."

### 0:35–0:44 — The reveal

This is the launch moment — the "something new" from the pinned graphic. Do this live, not a recording,
even if it's a little imperfect; imperfect-but-real beats a polished screen capture for a live audience.

> **Host:** "This is `pandaslp.com.ng` — live right now. Watch this."

Open `/agent/small-claims` on screen (or narrate from a second device if audio-only). Type a short,
real-sounding opener — reuse Obi's case or invite a **volunteer from the audience** to give a real
(or anonymized) scenario in the chat for you to type in live; that's a stronger moment than a canned demo.

> **Host, narrating as it responds:** "Notice it's not throwing a form at me — it's asking me one
> natural question at a time, the way [lawyer's name] just said a real lawyer would. ... And there —
> that's a court-ready Letter of Demand, drafted in the time we've been talking. From here a licensed
> reviewer — a real person, not the AI — checks it before anything goes out. From ₦10,000 if you serve
> it yourself, ₦25,000 if our team serves it for you."

> **Lawyer, closing the reveal:** "And to be clear — the AI drafts, but a licensed lawyer reviews and
> signs off before anything is served. That review step doesn't change tonight or ever."

(That review-before-release line is not just good messaging — it's operationally true as of last
weekend's release. Say it because it's real, not because it sounds reassuring.)

### 0:44–0:53 — Audience Q&A

Open the mic queue now. Seed it if it's quiet — have the co-host ask the first question from chat to
break the ice. Likely questions and how to field them:

- **"Does this work for [specific state]?"** → Small Claims Court Rules vary by state; confirm the
  agent asks for the state and applies the right rules — don't promise coverage you haven't verified.
- **"What if the person just ignores the letter?"** → That's step 3–5 above; reiterate the follow-up
  cadence (Day 0 → Day 3 → Day 7 → court) already covered.
- **"Is this actually a law firm or an app?"** → Direct, no hedging: CAC-registered business, licensed
  lawyers review and serve every document, notified the NBA. Never let "AI-native" read as "not a real
  firm" — that's the one brand risk worth being crisp about live.
- **Anything about a live/ongoing case** → Take it to DMs. Don't give specific legal advice on-air for
  someone's actual pending matter — that's a liability and compliance risk, not just a vibe thing.
