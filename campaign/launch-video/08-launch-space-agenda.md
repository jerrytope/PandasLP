## Vision & Mission — read this before anything else, MC

You're not the one making the case tonight — the founder and the lawyer are. But you're the one
keeping the whole hour pointed at *why* this exists, so every transition and every Q&A redirect still
lands on-message even when the conversation goes somewhere the script didn't predict.

**Vision:** A Nigeria where being owed money, or being shut out of a fair legal process, isn't
something people just absorb and move on from — because real legal help stopped being a luxury
reserved for those who can afford ₦50,000 and three weeks of waiting.

**Mission:** Pandas LP pairs AI-driven intake with licensed Nigerian lawyers so that starting a real
legal case — a demand letter, a court filing, a judgment enforced — takes minutes and costs from
₦10,000, not weeks and half a million naira. Every document is drafted by AI and reviewed, signed, and
stood behind by a real, licensed lawyer before it ever leaves the building.

**What this means for you as MC:**
- Every transition should trace back to one idea: the *wait* is the enemy — not the debtor, not the
  courts, the wait, and everything that grows in the space it leaves. If a tangent drifts off that,
  your job is the graceful redirect back to it, not to let it wander.
- When Q&A pushes on "is this really a law firm," don't field it yourself — throw it to the founder or
  lawyer, but know the answer well enough to recognize a good one: we exist specifically so licensed
  legal help stops being rationed by cost and time.
- Keep the AI in its place in how you frame every question — it drafts, it doesn't decide, sign, or
  replace the lawyer's review. Don't let a question imply otherwise without a correction landing.
- Your job tonight isn't to sell the product — it's to run a show tight enough that the founder and
  lawyer get to demonstrate the mission working, live, in front of witnesses.

### 0:20–0:37 — The journey, step by step

Hand this segment to the lawyer, MC moderating. Post the journey graphic in the Space's card / pin it
now if you haven't. Five beats, matching the graphic exactly — keep each to 2–3 minutes, plain
English, no statute citations unless someone asks:

| # | Step | MC's prompt for the lawyer |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Demand Letter** | "What does a real Letter of Demand actually need to say to hold up? What makes one weak?" |
| 02 | **File & Evidence** | "If the letter's ignored, what evidence actually matters — invoices, WhatsApp messages, what?" (Cue: WhatsApp messages are admissible — same line as the film.) |
| 03 | **Hearing** | "What's a Small Claims hearing actually like? Do you need to hire a barrister, show up in a wig?" (Answer should deflate the intimidation factor — that's the point.) |
| 04 | **Judgment** | "Say you win — then what? Does the money just appear?" |
| 05 | **Enforcement** | "This is the step nobody talks about — what happens when they still don't pay after judgment?" |

> **MC (transition after step 5):** "So that's the real journey — five steps, and most people never
> get past step one because starting it feels like a wall. [Founder Name], that's the exact wall you
> built something to remove — show us."

### 0:37–0:46 — The reveal

This is the launch moment — the "something new" from the pinned graphic. The founder drives it live,
not a recording, even if it's a little imperfect; imperfect-but-real beats a polished screen capture
for a live audience. MC's job here is to react and keep energy up, not to narrate the product himself.

> **Founder:** "This is `pandaslp.com.ng` — live right now. Watch this."

Open `/agent/small-claims` on screen (or narrate from a second device if audio-only). Type a short,
real-sounding opener — reuse Obi's case or invite a **volunteer from the audience** to give a real
(or anonymized) scenario in the chat for the founder to type in live; that's a stronger moment than a
canned demo.

> **Founder, narrating as it responds:** "Notice it's not throwing a form at me — it's asking one
> natural question at a time, the way [lawyer's name] just said a real lawyer would. ... And there —
> that's a court-ready Letter of Demand, drafted in the time we've been talking. From here a licensed
> reviewer — a real person, not the AI — checks it before anything goes out. From ₦10,000 if you serve
> it yourself, ₦25,000 if our team serves it for you."

> **Lawyer, closing the reveal:** "And to be clear — the AI drafts, but a licensed lawyer reviews and
> signs off before anything is served. That review step doesn't change tonight or ever."

(That review-before-release line is not just good messaging — it's operationally true as of last
weekend's release. Say it because it's real, not because it sounds reassuring.)

> **MC:** "That's happening live, right now, while we're talking. Let's open it up — if you've got
> questions, get in the queue."

### 0:46–0:55 — Audience Q&A (MC moderates)

Open the mic queue now. Seed it if it's quiet — have the producer ask the first question from chat to
break the ice. Likely questions and who should field them:

- **"Does this work for [specific state]?"** → Lawyer. Small Claims Court Rules vary by state; confirm
  the agent asks for the state and applies the right rules — don't promise coverage that hasn't been verified.
- **"What if the person just ignores the letter?"** → Lawyer, pointing back to steps 3–5: the Day 0 →
  Day 3 → Day 7 → court cadence already covered.
- **"Is this actually a law firm or an app?"** → Founder, direct, no hedging: CAC-registered business,
  licensed lawyers review and serve every document, notified the NBA. Never let "AI-native" read as
  "not a real firm" — that's the one brand risk worth being crisp about live.
- **Anything about a live/ongoing case** → MC redirects to DMs, on either guest's behalf if needed.
  Don't give specific legal advice on-air for someone's actual pending matter — that's a liability and
  compliance risk, not just a vibe thing.
