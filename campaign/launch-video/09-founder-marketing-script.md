# 09 — Founder's Space Script: "Your AI Legal Team" (solo speaking script)

**Use:** your own speaking part on the launch Space — what Pandas LP is, what you're building, and
what's launching tonight. Written to be delivered almost verbatim, but every section has a "the point
of this beat" note so you can go off-script and still land the same effect.

**Runtime:** ~10–12 minutes read straight through at a normal pace. Longer live once the room reacts.
**Goal of every single line:** get someone listening to open `pandaslp.com.ng` before the Space ends.

---

## Before you start talking

Three things to have open/ready:
1. A browser tab on `pandaslp.com.ng/agent/small-claims` — you're going to use this live, not describe it.
2. The UTM link ready to drop the moment you say it: `pandaslp.com.ng/agent/small-claims?utm_source=x&utm_campaign=launch-space`
3. Obi's numbers memorized cold, don't read them off a script: **₦450,000, 94 days, ₦10,000, 10 minutes.**
   Specific numbers said from memory sound true. Numbers read off a page sound like an ad.

---

## 1. The hook (0:00–0:30)

*The point of this beat: stop the scroll. If this doesn't land, nothing after it matters.*

> "Quick question before anything else — how much money do you think is owed to people in this room
> right now? Not owed to a bank. Owed to *you*, personally, by someone who said 'next week' and then
> just... stopped answering.
>
> Keep that number in your head. Because in the next ten minutes I'm going to show you something that
> gets it back — live, right now, while we're talking."

*(Pause. Let it sit for two full seconds before continuing. Silence is the most underused tool in a live talk.)*

---

## 2. The problem, made specific (0:30–2:00)

*The point of this beat: replace an abstract idea ("debt recovery is hard") with one vivid, true story
your audience can see themselves in. Abstractions don't convert. Specifics do.*

> "Let me tell you about a guy named Obi. Runs a small furniture workshop in Lagos. A client orders a
> full dining set — ₦450,000 — takes delivery, and says 'I'll pay you next week, I swear.'
>
> That was three months ago. Ninety-four days. Grey ticks on WhatsApp. Calls that just ring out.
>
> Now — Obi could call a lawyer. And here's what actually happens if he does: he pays ₦50,000 just to
> *start*, before anyone's written a single word. Then he waits two to four weeks for a demand letter.
> And here's the part nobody says out loud — most people, at that point, just... don't. They eat the
> loss. Not because they're wrong. Because the process to be right costs more than most people can
> justify spending on a maybe.
>
> That's not a legal problem. That's a *math* problem. And when getting what you're legally owed
> requires math you can't afford to lose, most people just absorb it. And that absorbed loss doesn't
> disappear — it becomes the next unpaid invoice, the next 'I'll figure it out myself,' the next person
> who decides handshake deals in this country just aren't safe anymore. That absorbed loss is what
> actually creates chaos. Not the lawsuits. The silence before them."

---

## 3. Why this is mine to fix (2:00–3:00)

*The point of this beat: credibility without a resume dump. One sentence of "who I am," then straight
into the belief — the belief is what people remember and repeat, not your job titles.*

> "I've spent my career building infrastructure most people never think about. At Dojah, identity
> verification — the thing that lets a fintech confirm you're really you in seconds instead of days.
> At Norebase, business registration — the paperwork that used to take weeks compressed into minutes.
>
> And somewhere in that work I started asking an obvious question: if verifying who you are, and
> registering a business, both got compressed from weeks to minutes — why hasn't *getting a lawyer to
> write one letter* changed at all? Why does that still cost ₦50,000 and take three weeks in 2026?
>
> I don't think most people who get stiffed on a debt are asking for anything complicated. They're
> asking for the process to actually be *available* to them. When law only reaches people who can
> already afford ₦50,000 and three weeks of waiting, that's not access to justice — that's justice
> rationed by income. And rationed justice is how you get a society where people quietly stop trusting
> each other's word. Making law accessible isn't charity. It's how you keep an economy honest."

---

## 4. The reveal — introduce Pandas LP (3:00–4:00)

*The point of this beat: name the thing, say what it is in one sentence a 10-year-old could repeat,
then immediately kill the "is this a real law firm" doubt before it forms.*

> "So here's what we built. It's called Pandas LP. One sentence: **an AI legal team that runs the exact
> interview a senior lawyer would run, drafts a court-ready Letter of Demand in about ten minutes, and
> then hands it to a real, licensed Nigerian lawyer who reviews it, signs off, and serves it.**
>
> Say that last part again in your head, because it matters: a *licensed lawyer* reviews and signs off
> on every single document before it ever leaves the building. Not a suggestion. Not a disclaimer.
> An actual person, an actual license, every time. Pandas LP is CAC-registered — Business Name
> BN 9646741 — and the Nigerian Bar Association has been notified. This is not an app that pretends to
> be a law firm. It's a law firm that happens to be very, very fast at the first step."

---

## 5. Live demo (4:00–7:00) — the highest-converting five minutes of the whole talk

*The point of this beat: nothing sells like watching it actually happen, in real time, with real
imperfections. A live demo that stumbles slightly and still works is more convincing than a smooth
recording — it proves you're not hiding anything.*

> "Let me just show you instead of telling you. This is `pandaslp.com.ng`, live, right now."

*(Open `/agent/small-claims`. Type Obi's situation in — or better, ask the room/chat for a real,
anonymized scenario and use theirs. Someone else's real problem, solved live, is the single strongest
moment available to you tonight.)*

> "Watch what it just asked me — it didn't throw a form at me. It asked one plain-English question,
> the way an actual lawyer opens a client conversation. ... And it just told me WhatsApp messages are
> admissible evidence under Nigerian law — that's not a canned line, that's the actual legal reasoning
> happening in real time.
>
> ... And there. That's a court-ready Letter of Demand. We've been talking for about as long as that
> took to draft. From here it goes to a licensed reviewer — a real person — who checks it, signs it,
> and serves it. From ₦10,000 if you serve it yourself. ₦25,000 if our team serves it for you and you
> never have to knock on anyone's door."

---

## 6. Kill the remaining objections before anyone has to ask (7:00–8:30)

*The point of this beat: the three doubts every skeptical listener is silently having. Naming a
doubt before someone raises it is more persuasive than defending against it after — it makes you look
like you already thought it through, not like you're back on your heels.*

> "Three things people usually think at this point, so let me just say them out loud.
>
> **'Is the AI actually deciding my case?'** No. It drafts. A licensed lawyer reviews, signs, and
> serves. That line doesn't move, tonight or ever — it's not a legal disclaimer, it's literally how
> the product is built.
>
> **'What if they just ignore the letter?'** Then we follow up — Day 3, Day 7 — and if they still
> won't pay, we file at Small Claims Court. You don't chase anyone. That's our job now, not yours.
>
> **'Is my information safe?'** Encrypted, Supabase-backed, and payment runs through Paystack — the
> same rails Nigerian banks and fintechs already trust with your money."

---

## 7. The price frame — make ₦10,000 feel obviously cheap (8:30–9:15)

*The point of this beat: a price alone is neutral. A price next to what it replaces is either cheap
or expensive — you control which one people feel by choosing the comparison.*

> "Say that number next to the other one: ₦50,000 and two to four weeks the traditional way, versus
> ₦10,000 and about ten minutes tonight. That's not a discount. That's a different category of thing."

---

## 8. Urgency + the single clearest instruction of the night (9:15–10:00)

*The point of this beat: people act on specific, immediate instructions, not vague enthusiasm. One
sentence, one action, said twice.*

> "If someone owes you money right now, here's the only thing I want you to remember: open
> pandaslp.com.ng, tap Small Claims, and just start talking to it like you would a lawyer. Ten minutes.
> From ₦10,000. No retainer.
>
> Do it tonight, while this is fresh — not next week, because next week is exactly the trap this whole
> talk has been about. The link's in the pinned post. I'm dropping it in chat right now."

*(Say the domain out loud, clearly, twice across the whole talk minimum. People need to hear a URL
more than once before it sticks.)*

---

## 9. The close (10:00–10:30)

*The point of this beat: one line people can repeat to someone else tomorrow. That's the actual
distribution mechanism — not this Space, the sentence someone says about it afterward.*

> "AI drafts it in minutes. Real lawyers make it count. That's Pandas LP — your AI legal team.
> Thank you for being here tonight."

---

## Delivery notes

- **Say the numbers, don't read them.** ₦450,000 / 94 days / ₦10,000 / 10 minutes said from memory,
  looking at nothing, lands as testimony. Read off a script, it lands as copy.
- **The live demo is the whole game.** If you only nail one section, make it that one — everything
  before it is setup, everything after it is closing the sale on something the room just watched happen.
- **Don't rush the pause after the hook.** The instinct under nerves is to fill silence. Fight it —
  two seconds of silence after "live, right now, while we're talking" is doing real work.
- **Say the URL twice, spoken slowly the second time.** Once in section 5, once in section 8 — a
  listener who tuned out the first time still catches the second.
- Everything here is written for you to speak alone. If the MC/lawyer structure in
  `08-launch-space-agenda.md` is also running that night, this script *is* your Founder segment —
  drop it into that run-of-show in place of the shorter placeholder lines, don't run both separately.
