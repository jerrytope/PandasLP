# 01 — Campaign Concept & Visual Direction

## The concept: “The Letter”

Every Nigerian knows the feeling of being owed. The promise on WhatsApp — *“I’ll pay you
next week, I swear”* — that turns into three months of silence. The campaign centres on one
object: **the Letter of Demand on Pandas LP letterhead**. It is the moment a private
frustration becomes a legal fact. The film follows one client from the sting of being ignored,
through a ten-minute conversation with Pandas AI, to the letter being served — and the money
landing.

**Why this concept:** it dramatizes the exact product (AI interview → court-ready letter →
lawyers serve and escalate), it fits the premium-and-trustworthy tone (calm authority, not
hype), and the letter itself is a beautiful, ownable visual — firm letterhead, panda mark,
amber seal on near-black.

## One-line promise

> AI drafts it in minutes. Real lawyers make it count.

Supported by the three proof points the site already leads with:
1. **10-minute process** — court-ready Letter of Demand from a plain-English conversation
2. **Real lawyers** — CAC-registered firm; licensed practitioners serve, file, and appear in court
3. **From ₦10,000** — transparent pricing vs ₦50,000+ and 2–4 weeks the traditional way

## Hero character sheet (paste verbatim into every Higgsfield Soul prompt)

> **OBI** — a Nigerian man in his mid-30s, dark brown skin, short neat afro fade with light
> beard, calm intelligent eyes, wearing a plain charcoal-grey short-sleeve shirt. He owns a
> small furniture workshop in Lagos filled with warm wood, hand tools, and sawdust in the air.

Secondary (one shot, for breadth): **AMAKA** — a Nigerian woman in her late 20s, braided hair
tied back, wearing a rust-orange blouse, a caterer boxing small chops in a bright commercial
kitchen in Lagos.

Keeping one hero keeps Higgsfield character consistency manageable; Amaka appears only in the
montage so drift doesn’t matter.

## Look & feel

- **Palette on screen mirrors the brand:** deep shadows crushed toward `#060605`; one warm
  amber practical light source (`#F5A623` family) in almost every frame — a workshop bulb,
  a phone glow, the letterhead seal. Green (`#3ECF8E`) appears exactly twice: the credit
  alert and the “✓ Served” status. Scarcity keeps it meaningful.
- **Cinematography:** slow, deliberate camera — dolly-ins and crane moves, never handheld
  whip. Shallow depth of field. 24fps feel. This is a law firm, not a crypto app.
- **Nigeria is specific, not generic:** Lagos skyline at dusk, naira notes, WhatsApp UI,
  danfo yellow blurred in a background — real texture, never poverty-porn.
- **Typography on screen:** Bricolage Grotesque for supers (bold, -0.03em tracking, white),
  DM Mono for small “evidence” labels (timestamps, prices, `DAY 3 OF 7`).
- **The letter** is always shot like a hero product: raking amber light across cotton paper,
  the panda monogram embossed, signature in ink.

## What we never do

- No gavel-and-marble stock-lawyer clichés, no suited actor pointing at the camera
- No “AI” visual clichés — no glowing blue brains, no robot hands
- No overpromising: the film shows a demand letter working; court is “if they still won’t pay”
- ₦ figures shown match real pricing (from ₦10,000) and the site’s demo case (₦450,000)
